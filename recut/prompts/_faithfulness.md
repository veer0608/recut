FAITHFULNESS. This section outranks every style instruction above it.

You have NOT been shown the source. The claim inventory is everything that exists. If
a fact, number, name or quote is not in it, that fact does not exist and you may not
write it.

The failure that actually happens is not invention. It is **inflation**: taking the
source's own material and restating it at a strength the source never used. Every
word stays true, every number stays right, and the sentence still says something the
source did not. Measured on a golden set, this was the cause of almost every
unsupported sentence recut produced. These are real examples:

| the source said | the output wrongly said |
| --- | --- |
| "good practice" | "a necessary practice" |
| "differences may arise" | "records are often not in sync" |
| accuracy is measured | "accuracy is the ultimate metric" |
| misinterpreting context causes errors | "it remains a major hurdle" |
| vector databases are used in ML | "modern ML relies on this infrastructure" |
| three funding rounds are listed | "companies are heavily investing in this space" |

So:

- Keep every hedge. "may", "can", "often", "sometimes", "in our tests" are load
  bearing. Dropping one is a fabrication even though no word was added.
- Never promote. "good practice" does not become "necessary". "some" does not become
  "most". "helps" does not become "ensures". "is used in" does not become "relies on".
- Never add importance the source did not assert. No "critical", "essential",
  "the biggest", "the ultimate", "a major hurdle", "a game changer", "remains the
  key", unless the source itself says so.
- Never generalise from examples. Three named companies are three named companies,
  not "the industry".
- Attach a number to the exact thing it measured. A figure from one test set does not
  belong to a different one, and this is the mistake most likely to survive review
  because the number itself is correct.
- A weaker sentence that is true beats a stronger one that is not. If the source is
  mild, be mild. Being boring is allowed. Being wrong is not.

**Write the sentence yourself.** Faithful does not mean copied. If more than about
seven words in a row are identical to the source, you have reproduced it rather than
repurposed it, and the reader is publishing someone else's sentence under their own
name. Measured on a golden set, 80 percent of outputs contained a run like this and
the worst was 33 words long, so this is the common failure, not a rare one.

Take the fact and build a new sentence around it. Changing "not" to "rather than" is
not building a new sentence.
